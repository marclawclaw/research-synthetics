# CDP on LEZ: evidence on admin control

This note holds the evidence behind Part 1 ("The rules") of the Notion page "CDP-Backed Synthetics": who can change each rule in comparable protocols, what the relevant Logos RFPs specify, and what LEZ provides for program upgrades. It holds no decisions; those are on the page. The map from page parts to vault notes is in [[rfp/appendix/cdp-notion-map]]. Evidence for the protocol comparisons is also in [[patterns/cdp-synthetics-landscape]] (sections 4a and 4b) and the admin-control sections of [[projects/liquity]], [[projects/sky-maker]], [[projects/synthetix-v2]], [[projects/frankencoin]] and [[projects/crvusd]]. Written 2026-11-16; fact-checked and reduced to evidence on 2026-11-17.

## Who can change each rule: ecosystem overview

Rows are the levers listed in Part 1 of the page. Each cell answers: who can change this rule, how fast, and within what limits. Evidence tags: **chain** = read from Ethereum mainnet on 2026-11-16 unless dated otherwise; **source** = the contract source code; **docs** = the project's documentation; **vault** = these research notes. The full lever table of each protocol is in its note; the matrix keeps one category per cell and the fact that decides it. "Not checked" means we did not look, not that the rule cannot be changed.

Category key (a cell's category is taken from the first line of its entry; where the cell names a further power, the category does not show it):

- **Nobody:** fixed in code, or no such power.
- **Borrowers or anyone, within rules fixed in code.**
- **Equity-token holders:** holders of the protocol's equity token (Frankencoin: a qualified holder can propose or veto).
- **Token vote.**
- **Token vote plus a faster path:** a multisig or a bounded setter that can also change it.
- **Multisig alone:** no vote on chain.

### General governance, per protocol

- **Liquity V1:** no governance. Owners of the core contracts are the zero address (chain).
- **Liquity V2:** no governance over protocol rules; contracts are immutable. Governance only directs where the 25% interest stream goes. LQTY stakers vote in 7 day epochs (weighting by stake age is from the vault note, not re-read); the governance contract's owner is zero and it has no role in any core contract (chain, source).
- **Sky/Maker:** SKY holders vote in Chief V3. `launch()` was a one-time switch that turned the Chief on once 2.4B SKY backed the zero address; `live()` is now 1 (chain, 2026-11-17; source: `sky-ecosystem/chief` `Chief.sol`). A new spell must outvote the current hat, which had about 7.03B SKY of approval on 2026-11-16 ([[projects/sky-maker]]); only the hat can schedule into the 48 hour delay. The Chief holds 7.14B of 23.45B SKY (30.4%) (chain). Bounded fast paths sit beside it: SP-BEAM (2 of 3 Safe; borrow rates and SSR within 2% to 30%, DSR 0% to 30%), AutoLine (debt ceilings, anyone can trigger) and the stop-only emergency modules (chain). Who the large SKY voters are was not checked.
- **Synthetix V2:** a Safe multisig, 4 of 7 signers, with no delay, guard or modules, still in use (nonce 3,370 on 2026-11-17, fact-check read, not repeated) (chain). A search-result summary says Synthetix's developer docs name that address as the Protocol DAO multisig (the page itself returned 404; unverified). Stakers elect councils that vote on proposals (docs, unverified), but nothing on chain binds the Safe to a vote. SIP-124 set "a minimum of five signatures (configurable via SCCP)", so 4 of 7 may reflect an approved change rather than a conflict (the SCCP was not found). SIP-401, which would have replaced the DAO with a Risk Council, was rejected.
- **Frankencoin:** no owner, admin or proxy (source). Rule changes need a qualified holder: 2% of time-weighted FPS votes acting directly, or more than 1% of FCS votes acting through the 2026 FCS modules; the FCS contract holds 35% of FPS votes, so its decisions clear the 2% bar (chain). Delegation is transitive, and a holder can sacrifice votes to destroy another's. The audit's trust model assumes no malicious majority of votes.
- **crvUSD:** the Curve DAO, by on-chain vote: 7 days, 51% support, 30% quorum, then no delay, executed through the Aragon Agent and the factory's admin forwarder (chain). Next to it, the Emergency DAO (a 5 of 9 Safe, no guard or modules) can act at once: cut debt ceilings, raise loan discounts, switch the Chainlink clamp, kill PegKeepers, swap a market's rate contract or rewards callback (`set_callback`) and kill gauges (`set_killed`) (source, chain; [[projects/crvusd]]). The voter set was not checked.

What the table shows: Liquity V1 and V2 have no rule anyone can change. Sky/Maker and crvUSD put most rules behind a public vote with a delay, but both add faster paths: Sky through bounded, rate-limited setters and stop-only modules, crvUSD through a 5 of 9 Emergency DAO with immediate powers, most of them only able to reduce risk. Synthetix V2 puts every rule behind one multisig with no delay. Frankencoin replaces most rules with a veto, so the proposer's choices are cheap and holders' attention is the scarce resource.

An immutable core does not remove every admin. Liquity's own contracts have none, but it inherits Chainlink's (a 4 of 9 multisig can repoint the ETH/USD feed) and, in V2, Lido's and Rocket Pool's control over the exchange rates. Every surveyed oracle brings its own admin.

### Do risk changes reach open positions?

- **Sky/Maker and Synthetix V2:** yes; a spell or the Safe changes parameters that apply to open positions ([[projects/sky-maker]], [[projects/synthetix-v2]]).
- **crvUSD:** debt ceilings, rates and the AMM fee apply at once; a new liquidation discount does not. Each borrower's liquidation discount is stored when they last change their own loan, so it reaches a loan only when its borrower next touches it ([[projects/crvusd]], Risk parameters row).
- **Morpho Blue:** the owner cannot change an existing market's LLTV, oracle or rate model. It can set an existing market's fee with `setFee(marketParams, newFee)`, capped at `MAX_FEE` = 25% of interest (source: `morpho-org/morpho-blue` `Morpho.sol` and `ConstantsLib.sol`, read 2026-11-17).

## Admin structures elsewhere (precedent findings)

From a precedent audit run in the Notion session on 2026-11-17 by a research agent (vault notes and the docs of Aave V3, Morpho Blue and Vault V2, Alchemix, Lido, Balancer, OpenZeppelin, Indigo, Monolith and RedStone), plus the re-reads marked below. Agent-reported unless marked.

- **Delay enforced inside the contract:** found in Morpho Vault V2 (per-function timelocks, zero allowed; increasing a timelock is itself timelocked; decreasing uses the delay of the function being decreased; abdication sets a function's timelock to infinity) ([Morpho timelock docs](https://docs.morpho.org/curate/concepts/timelock), re-read 2026-11-17). Aave runs 1 day and 7 day executors by action class. A CDP core that enforces a delay: not found. A delay fixed at deployment: not found (Vault V2's curator can change it through a timelocked step). Maker's 48 hours is an external pause contract.
- **No delay, failure case:** Drift lost about $285M to $295M through a 2 of 5 council with no delay ([[projects/drift]]; the TRM Labs source says zero timelock). Synthetix V2's Safe also has no delay.
- **Stop-only and self-expiring pauses:** Aave V3 `setReserveFreeze`; the crvUSD Emergency DAO ceiling cut ([[projects/crvusd]]); Sky's stop-only emergency modules, unpaused by a 48 hour spell ([[projects/sky-maker]]); a self-expiring pause in Lido's CircuitBreaker and Balancer V2's pause window. Lido: "The mainnet 21-day initial pause duration"; the DAO can set a new duration for later triggers, within `MIN_PAUSE_DURATION` and `MAX_PAUSE_DURATION` (5 to 60 days per the earlier read); the pauser cannot choose the duration ([Lido CircuitBreaker docs](https://docs.lido.fi/contracts/circuit-breaker), re-read 2026-11-17). All of these limits in one role: not found. Sky has no guardian multisig.
- **Per-role controllers:** found in Aave V3 ACLManager, Morpho Vault V2 roles and Alchemix (Admin, Sentinel, Keeper). Maker's `End.cage()` is auth-gated, but the ESM threshold is set to the maximum, so only a spell can shut down ([[projects/sky-maker]]).
- **Per-market governors:** found in lending only (Euler V2 per-vault governors, Morpho curators), not in minting protocols.
- **Oracle signer sets held by the provider, not the protocol:** Sky does not control Chronicle's signers ([[projects/sky-maker]], Oracle row); Synthetix V2's Safe picks the Chainlink feed and stale period only. A protocol role that holds the signer set: not found.
- **Bounds in code that bind the controller:** Sky SP-BEAM bounds and rate limits (chain). Synthetix V2's caps did not bind its owner, who could swap the settings contract (source). Morpho Blue's owner can enable new rate models and LLTVs, set a market's fee (at most 25%), set the fee recipient and pass ownership, but cannot change an existing market's risk parameters ([[patterns/cdp-vs-morpho-trust]]; source as above).
- **Two-step ownership transfer (nominate, accept):** found in Synthetix's owned contracts ([[projects/synthetix-v2]]); not audited for other protocols.
- **Events on every change:** not checked per protocol.
- **A front end showing each lever's effective control, given upgradeability:** not found in any surveyed protocol.

## Logos RFPs

Re-read 2026-11-17 from the local clone of `logos-co/rfp` at `920fad9` (2026-11-06, same as `origin/master`).

- [RFP-001 Admin Authority Library](https://github.com/logos-co/rfp/blob/master/RFPs/RFP-001-admin-authority-lib.md): admin authority "is set at program initialisation"; it "can transfer admin authority to a new signer" (one call; no two-step transfer is required); it "can revoke admin authority, effectively renouncing admin control"; it is "the only one that can call privileged instructions"; "There can only be one admin authority (signer) at a time" (so one authority per program, not per role); soft requirement: it "can only be set to a valid new signer (on-curve key or deployed PDA)".
- [RFP-002 Freeze Authority Library](https://github.com/logos-co/rfp/blob/master/RFPs/RFP-002-freeze-authority-lib.md): a freeze authority, set at initialisation and changed or revoked by the admin authority, that can freeze the program, "rejecting any attempt to interact with it; apart from unfreezing or changing the freeze authority", or a specific `AccountId`. A full freeze therefore also blocks repaying, adding collateral and liquidating; a per-account freeze can stop one borrower from repaying.
- [RFP-005 Multisig and Coordination](https://github.com/logos-co/rfp/blob/master/RFPs/RFP-005-multisig-and-coordination.md): a multisig for LEZ programs.
- [RFP-020 RedStone Off-Chain Oracle Adaptor](https://github.com/logos-co/rfp/blob/master/RFPs/RFP-020-redstone-oracle-adaptor.md): a push aggregator writing an RFP-019 price account (the only read path for private execution) and a public-mode pull; m of N signers ("typically 3-of-N", set per feed); per-feed `maxAge`; feeds and signer sets managed by an RFP-001 admin authority, an upstream admin any user of the feed inherits. Detail in [[rfp/appendix/cdp-oracle]].

## What LEZ provides

Read 2026-11-17 from the local clone of `logos-blockchain/logos-execution-zone`, branch `dev`, commit `cb075d5` (2026-11-13, same as `origin/dev`); paths below are relative to it.

**Deployment and immutability.**

- The program loader has shipped since commit `283c9700f` (2026-09-03), in every tag from `v0.2.5-rc1` to `v0.4.0-rc3`; the 2026-10-01 clone has it at `lez/programs/program_loader/core`. Only `system_upgrader` (commit `7e0767c22`, 2026-11-12), the `CreateHeaderOf` and `AssertMutable` messages and the move to `lee/program_loader` are on `dev` only.
- A user program is deployed through the loader (`lee/program_loader/src/lib.rs`) as a header account pointing to a chain of bytecode segments. `ProgramHeader` carries only `image_id`, `program_first_segment` and `immutable: bool` ("Once `true`, this header can never be updated again"; `lee/state_machine/core/src/program/mod.rs:560-568`). It "lives at whatever account address the deployer chose", and each deployment is "a distinct instance for dispatch, PDA-derivation, and ownership purposes" (`mod.rs:553-558`).
- `UpdateHeader` asserts the old header is mutable and that the header account is authorised (`lib.rs:254-269`); it can also flip `immutable` to true (test `update_header_flip_to_immutable_lands_immutable_mirror_commitment` in `lee/state_machine/src/state/tests/deploy.rs:478`). Deploying immutable and making a program immutable later are both native, and immutability is enforced by the loader (`lib.rs:261-264`).

**Who can upgrade.**

- There is no upgrade-authority field. The upgrader is whoever can authorise the header account: either the transaction is signed by that account's key, or the sending program derives the account as its own PDA from a seed it presents (`lee/state_machine/core/src/execution_state.rs:1431-1470`, `353-358`). The PDA grant is inherited down the call tree (`execution_state.rs:1361-1366`, `1461`). User programs are not restricted from holding a header at their PDA: the loader rejects only the native token and loader addresses as targets (`lib.rs:285-296`).
- The PDA route is used today: `system_upgrader` holds every system program's header at its own PDA and upgrades it through a chained `UpdateHeader` carrying the PDA seed (`lez/programs/system_upgrader/src/main.rs:3-7`, `169-172`). Tests: `lez/programs/src/lib.rs:568`, `604`, `699`; `integration_tests/tests/program_deployment/system_upgrade.rs:265`, `319`; `lez/sequencer/core/src/tests.rs:2546`, `7363`. No test covers a user program or a multisig holding a header.
- Upgrade authority cannot be rotated. The program ID is the header address, and an upgrade keeps that ID, its PDAs and its state (`system_upgrade.rs:265`). A public account address is a hash of its key (`lee/state_machine/src/signature/public_key.rs:76-85`), so a key-held header stays with that key. The only way to change who upgrades is to put the header at a controller program's PDA, and that controller must itself be immutable for the arrangement to hold.
- System programs (clock, fee, bridge and others) are installed and upgraded by a threshold of the sequencer committee with a scheduled activation height; `system_upgrader` is seeded immutable and only a hard fork changes it (`lez/programs/system_upgrader/README.md`).

**Privacy.**

- A header cannot be private: loader messages are refused outside public execution, so "no code upgrade may land between a proof's image claims and the transitions it covers" (`execution_state.rs:1172`).
- Calling a mutable program privately requires naming it and its image; the claim is checked at settlement, so an upgrade between proof and settlement invalidates the proof. Only immutable programs allow the `Undisclosed` image claim (`lee/state_machine/core/src/circuit_io.rs:84-86`; `lee/state_machine/src/validated_state_diff/mod.rs:677-688`).

**Tooling and other components.**

- The wallet and CLI support only a key-held header (`--header`, `--immutable`; `lez/wallet/src/cli/programs/program_loader.rs`). Their help text says immutable is "not protocol-enforced" (`program_loader.rs:51`, `67`), which is wrong: `lib.rs:261-264` enforces it.
- No mechanism to freeze a user program was found (keyword search for freeze, frozen, blacklist and denylist in `lee` and `lez`; not an audit).
- An LEZ multisig exists: `logos-co/lez-multisig` (modelled on Squads v4: create, propose, approve M of N, execute through `ChainedCall` to any program). Its last commit `a057fc4` (2026-03-31) depends on `nssa_core` from `lssa` rev `767b5af`, which predates the loader, and it has no loader integration.
- Outdated documents: the LIP `lee-v0.3-specifications.md:1389-1412` (program ID derived from the bytecode) and `logos-docs` `admin-authority.md:124`, `444` describe the pre-loader model.
