# CDP and Debt-Pool Synthetics: Landscape (live, dying and dead)

Survey of 50 protocol rows (Monolith and Swan DAO share one row; Kamino kUSD is listed but not counted) of protocols that let users lock collateral and mint a synthetic token or stablecoin tracking an external reference (USD, EUR, JPY, BTC, ETH, equities, commodities). Access date for every row and every figure unless stated otherwise: 2026-11-16. TVL and supply are DefiLlama figures from that day ([protocols](https://api.llama.fi/protocols), [stablecoins](https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoins), [fees](https://api.llama.fi/overview/fees)); per-row source links give the protocol API page plus the primary documentation used for ratios, liquidation and fees. `[NOT FOUND]` means no sourced value was located; nothing here is estimated.

## 1. Four designs that are often conflated

| Design | Who carries the synthetic's risk | Liquidation | Peg anchor | Examples |
|--------|----------------------------------|-------------|------------|----------|
| Debt pool (shared liability) | All stakers collectively: each staker's debt is a share of the aggregate USD value of every outstanding synth, so it moves with trader PnL | Per staker, on the staker's own C-ratio, not on the price of a specific synth | No redemption at par. Structural bid: stakers must buy and burn the exchange unit (sUSD) to repay debt and claim rewards | Synthetix V1/V2 ([[projects/synthetix-v2]]); V3 pools ([[projects/synthetix-v3]]) |
| Per-position CDP | The individual borrower: debt equals what they minted; liabilities are not pooled | Per position, on its own CR (auction, Stability Pool, soft liquidation or step-in) | Redemption or repayment at $1 plus arbitrage, PSM, interest-rate or fee response | Sky/Maker, Liquity, crvUSD, Frankencoin, Youves, Metronome, Alchemix, Mirror |
| Collateral-split (dual token) | A leveraged "reserve" or xASSET holder absorbs all price moves so the stable side stays at $1 while the reserve ratio is healthy | None per user; collective reserve-ratio rules and fee curves | Mint and redeem at $1 only inside a reserve-ratio band, otherwise discounts | Djed, SigmaUSD, Money on Chain, Hylo, f(x) |
| Delta-neutral (out of scope here except as contrast) | The hedging counterparty (perp venue, funding rate) | None; the hedge is closed instead | Spot collateral hedged with a short perp; yield is funding | Ethena USDe ([[projects/ethena-usde]]), UXD, Hermetica USDh |

Notes on the boundaries:
- Debt pool versus LP-pool counterparty: Synthetix stakers are the pooled counterparty to all synth traders, which is the same economic exposure as GMX or Jupiter LPs described in [[patterns/lp-counterparty-risk]], but entry is through collateral lock-up and debt, not a deposit.
- Metronome Synth is a per-position CDP that mints several synths per position, but its swap module mints and burns synths at oracle price against a shared pool, so it carries a debt-pool-like exposure that produced the July 2026 shortfall ([[projects/metronome-synth]]).
- Alchemix removes the repayment burden (yield repays the loan) but each position is still its own debt; the Transmuter queue is the peg anchor ([[projects/alchemix]]).
- Collateral-split designs have no borrower and therefore no interest rate; the cost of the leverage side is the "borrower cost" (xASSET funding, fee curves).

## 3. What correlates with survival and failure

These are observations across 50 protocols, not causal claims; many causes overlap.

### 3.1 Failure patterns

| Pattern | Examples | Evidence |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Collateral or liquidity denominated in the protocol's own ecosystem asset (endogenous collateral) | Mirror (UST and LUNA collateral), Kujira USK (KUJI-linked Foundation leverage), Synthetix V1/V2 (SNX), Iron (TITAN) | Mirror: UST depeg May 2022 then oracle exploit ([[projects/mirror-protocol]]). Kujira: August 2024 Foundation liquidation then discontinuation. Synthetix: governance cut target C-ratio from 800% to 350 to 400% as SNX fell ([[projects/synthetix-v2]]) |
| Oracle-priced minting outside the liquidation path | Metronome swap module (July 2026), Mirror (LUNA price after fork) | About 6,367 msETH and 4.57M msUSD unbacked; msUSD $0.818 ([[projects/metronome-synth]]) |
| Upgradeable or complex periphery contracts | Abracadabra (CauldronV4 rounding $6.5M 2024-01-30, gmCauldrons $13M 2025-03-25, cook() flaw $1.7M 2025-10-04), Prisma (MigrateTroveZap $11.6M), Raft ($3.3M mint bug) | Abracadabra ended with about $21M bad debt and MIM at $0.0286 |
| Thin demand versus operating cost | Inter Protocol (sunset rationale: declining appetite, rising cost), Angle (declining activity), Lista CDP (migrated to lending), UXD | Forum and press statements cited in the table |
| Team exit or abandonment | Parrot, Hubble (docs offline), Lybra, Kolibri | No shutdown notices found |
| Single-venue key risk | Sovryn Zero (halted after sibling product exploits, 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01), Synthetify (governance attack on inactive DAO) | DefiLlama hacks list plus on-chain halt |

### 3.2 Survival patterns

- Immutable or near-immutable core, one or two collateral families and a hard redemption floor: Liquity V1 (TVL $130.3M after peaking at $4.52B) and Liquity V2. Counter-cases: Gravita and Prisma copied the Liquity model and failed or depegged, and Felix (a Liquity V2 fork) holds $17.8M against a $294.1M peak, so the model alone does not guarantee retention.
- Multi-collateral governance with large off-chain risk teams and diversified revenue: Sky/Maker ($3.76B TVL, $189.2M revenue over the past year). The failure (Black Thursday) came from auction design and stale oracles and was redesigned as Dutch auctions.
- Novel liquidation mechanisms that reduce forced selling: crvUSD LLAMMA soft liquidation (TVL $73.6M), Frankencoin oracle-free challenges ($48.3M and a record TVL in 2026-09), Alchemix same-asset debt (V2) and then 90% LTV with Transmuter (V3).
- Orderly shutdown tools: Inter Protocol raised CR by 50% per week then closed by 2025-07-01; Angle offers 1:1 redemption to 2027-03-01; Mars reduced liquidation LTVs to zero; Kujira used rate hikes. In these cases orderly exits coincide with an accountable team and a redemption or migration path; the disorderly exits (Mirror, MIM) had neither.
- Peg anchor choice: hard redemption at par (LUSD $1.003, BOLD $0.9985, Mezo MUSD $0.9929, Thala MOD $0.998) held tighter pegs than soft anchors (Alchemix queue $0.9666, MIM $0.0286, Metronome $0.818) on 2026-11-16 per DefiLlama prices, with exceptions (Gravita GRAI $0.4057 despite redemption).

### 3.3 Debt-pool versus per-position versus delta-neutral: implications

- Debt-pool designs concentrate counterparty risk in stakers and need a native or governance-set collateral token; Synthetix V1/V2 peaked at $2.97B TVL and paid $81.3M of cumulative exchange fees to stakers, then lost its TVL when solo staking was retired. The fee stream fell from $33.8M (2023) to $1.75M (2025) as exchange volume and TVL declined.
- Per-position CDPs give clean accounting (each debt is collateralised) but need an external peg anchor; the survivors all have one.
- Collateral-split designs (Djed, Hylo, f(x)) remove liquidations but make the stable side depend on the reserve ratio; Djed has been below its 400% minting floor since mid-2023 (derived ratio about 289% on 2026-11-16); Hylo and f(x) are young and volatile (f(x) TVL fell 58% in ten days in November 2026).
- Delta-neutral designs (Ethena, UXD) have no borrower and no CR; their risk is funding and venue risk, a separate category. UXD wound down; see [[projects/ethena-usde]] for the live example.

## 4b. Frankencoin and crvUSD: axis 2 and axis 3 detail (read 2026-11-16)

Method as in 4a (`eth_call` and `eth_getStorageAt` through `ethereum-rpc.publicnode.com`; `eth_getLogs` over wide ranges was refused by the endpoint, so event history was not scanned). Governance rules below are from each project's documentation unless marked "chain".

**Frankencoin (ZCHF), Ethereum.**
- Axis 3 (chain): ZCHF, FPS and FCS expose no `owner()` or `admin()` getter and have no EIP-1967 implementation or admin slot. Absence of a getter is not proof that no privileged role exists: I did not decompile bytecode or read source, and the ChainSecurity audit scope lists an `Ownable.sol`, which I could not place (most plausibly per-position ownership; unverified).
- Axis 2 (docs): anyone can propose a new position with the collateral token, liquidation price, risk premium over the global rate, term and limits; the fee is 1,000 ZCHF (not refunded after a veto) and the initialisation period is at least three days, during which a qualified holder can veto. Minters are added by `suggestMinter`, which any address may call, subject to the same veto. Current docs set the veto threshold at more than 1% of time-weighted voting power ("exactly 1% is not enough"); older secondary sources and the MiCA classification PDF say 2%.
- Trust shifts from an admin key to token holders: the veto is plutocratic and time-weighted, with `attack()` letting a holder destroy another holder's votes, and delegation is transitive. The audit's trust model "assumes no malicious majority of internal FCS votes after binding", and the docs say FCS governance "can become usable before the wrapper is binding" (what binding requires is not explained).
- Docs and chain disagree: docs describe `MinterGovernance.suggestMinter` with a 60 day application period and 1,200 ZCHF fee; the ZCHF contract at the documented address returns `MIN_APPLICATION_PERIOD()` = 14 days and `MIN_FEE()` = 1,000 ZCHF. Possibly the docs describe a newer module on the FCS side; unresolved. The docs also list no MintingHub or PositionFactory address, so I did not read those contracts or the minter set.
- Cross-chain: the docs mention "CCIPAdmin rules" for cross-chain configuration without describing them, so a cross-chain admin role may exist.
- ZCHF is pegged to CHF and carries no oracle price for liquidation (liquidation price is proposer-set; challenges are oracle-free per docs), so the oracle risk that hit Ostium, Metronome and Mirror is structurally absent; the cost is that collateral selection is entirely a share-holder veto problem.

**crvUSD, Ethereum.**
- Axis 3 (chain): `crvUSD.minter()` is the ControllerFactory. `factory.admin()` is a 22-function contract (identified selectors: `dao()`, `set_monetary_policy(address,address)`, `set_callback(address,address)`; the other 19 not identified) whose `dao()` returns the Curve DAO Aragon Agent. Curve DAO voting runs for 7 days with 51% support and 30% quorum. The nine market controllers read (sfrxETH, wstETH and LBTC checked individually) have no EIP-1967 proxy slot, but each points to a swappable `monetary_policy()` contract, and the admin path has a `set_monetary_policy` function, so the DAO can change the interest-rate logic of existing markets (function-level access control not read).
- Axis 2 (docs and chain): nine collaterals; markets are added through the DAO-controlled factory, not by users (I did not confirm the access control on `add_market` from source). This is the Aave end of axis 2, in contrast to Frankencoin.
- Not checked: the Curve DAO's token distribution and voting concentration (veCRV), the Aragon Agent's own upgradeability beyond noting it is a kernel-managed Aragon app proxy, and whether any off-chain guardian can pause crvUSD minting (the Curve Emergency DAO exists but I did not tie it to crvUSD).

**Reading across the three axes.**

| | Who can use it | Who can add collateral or markets | Who can change the rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synthetix V2 | anyone | governance, via a 4-of-7 key | the same 4-of-7 Safe, no delay |
| crvUSD | anyone | Curve DAO vote (7 days) | Curve DAO vote (7 days) |
| Sky/Maker | anyone | governance (executive votes) through a 48 hour pause | same |
| Liquity V1 | anyone | fixed at deployment; no one | no one (renounced) |
| Frankencoin | anyone | anyone proposes; token holders veto | token-holder veto; no admin getter found |

Frankencoin is the only surveyed design that is permissionless on axis 2 and keyless on axis 3 as far as these reads go, which makes it the closest Morpho-like analogue in the CDP family. The caveats above (docs and chain disagree; veto power concentrates with long-term share holders; the minter set and position contracts were not read) are what separate "closest analogue" from "verified permissionless".
